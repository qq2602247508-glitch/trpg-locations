# Waterdeep morphology notes

Round 13 uses these notes only to derive original procedural rules. It does not copy a published map, street coordinates, encounter layout, or artwork.

## Public sources

- [Waterdeep: Dragon Heist source index](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/wdh) — confirms that Waterdeep is described through a long history, distinct wards, city life, landmarks, and a poster map. The generator uses the ideas of historical layering and ward identity, not the map itself.
- [Urban Intrigue in Waterdeep: Dragon Heist](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/307-urban-intrigue-in-waterdeep-dragon-heist) — describes sharply different ward moods: muddy, rough Dock Ward; cleaner North Ward; opulent Sea Ward. This supports morphology and material differences between wards instead of one shared grid.
- [Lords of Waterdeep rulebook](https://media.wizards.com/downloads/dnd/DnD_LOW_Rulebook_EN.pdf) — identifies the deep-water basin as the source of the city's name and wealth and associates the harbor with Dock Ward. This supports a harbor-first rather than road-grid-first layout.

## Procedural deductions

These are design inferences from the public descriptions, not copied cartography.

1. **Harbor first.** The shoreline, piers, quays and cargo handling band are primary constraints. Streets bend with the usable shore and connect inland anchors to working piers.
2. **Ward identity changes morphology.** Dock-side blocks should be steep, dense and confusing; civic or noble wards can have broader streets and larger plots. A single rectilinear grammar is inappropriate.
3. **Historical accretion.** An old route between gate, market and harbor precedes later lanes. Secondary streets branch from it, old wall fragments become property boundaries, and later expansion leaves offset junctions.
4. **Mixed use, not modern zoning.** Warehouses, tenements, taverns, poorhouses and small businesses interleave, with the heaviest cargo buildings closest to quays.
5. **Wayfinding by landmarks.** Towers, temples, markets, gates, cliff/mountain silhouettes and large civic masses should orient the road graph.
6. **Tactical verticality.** Rooftops, steep streets, stairs, second-storey bridges and waterfront level changes should connect to real standable surfaces.

## Generated Waterdeep-style harbor contract

- segmented, seed-dependent shoreline;
- 4–8 piers with varied headings and lengths;
- one curving quay/cargo spine following the shore;
- 2–4 inland anchor routes to market, ward gate, shrine and watch post;
- narrow lanes branching from those routes instead of an even grid;
- irregular blocks generated from local road bands;
- narrow shared-wall housing deeper inland;
- warehouses and yards in the cargo band;
- fish market between piers and commercial lanes;
- taverns and lodging along high-traffic transitions;
- at least one visible tower/temple/civic landmark;
- roof route generated only when both endpoints have reachable roof surfaces.

## Explicit non-goals

- no recreation of the published poster map;
- no named street coordinate database;
- no direct translation of copyrighted encounter maps;
- no claim that generated layouts are canonical Waterdeep geography.
